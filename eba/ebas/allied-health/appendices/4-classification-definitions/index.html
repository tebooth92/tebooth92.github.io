    SECTION A – General Definitions¶

    Definitions¶

    In this classification structure, the following terms are defined as follows:

    1. 1.1 Advanced Practice is defined in clause 1 of Schedule 4 of this Appendix 4.
    2. 1.2 AHP1 Classification/s means the following professions:
      1. (a) Art Therapist;
      2. (b) Cardiac Technologist (Cardiac Physiologist);
      3. (c) Dental Prosthetist (from commencement of the Agreement);
      4. (d) Exercise Physiologist;
      5. (e) Health Information Manager (Medical Records Administrator);
      6. (f) Health Promotion Officer (Health Promotion Practitioner) (from commencement of the Agreement);
      7. (g) Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer);
      8. (h) Medical Librarian;
      9. (i) Music Therapist;
      10. (j) Nuclear Medicine Technologist;
      11. (k) Occupational Therapist;
      12. (l) Orthoptist;
      13. (m) Orthotist/Prosthetist;
      14. (n) Photographer or Illustrator (Medical Photographer or Illustrator);
      15. (o) Physiotherapist;
      16. (p) Play Therapist (Child Life Therapist);
      17. (q) Podiatrist;
      18. (r) Radiation Therapy Technologist (Radiation Therapist);
      19. (s) Recreation Therapist;
      20. (t) Social Worker;
      21. (u) Sonographer; and
      22. (v) Speech Pathologist.
    3. 1.3 AHP2 Classification/s means the following professions:
      1. (a) Biomedical Technologist;
      2. (b) Child Psychotherapist;
      3. (c) Client Advisor/Rehabilitation Consultant;
      4. (d) Community Development Worker;
      5. (e) Dental Prosthetist (until commencement of the Agreement);
      6. (f) Dental Technician;
      7. (g) Medical Laboratory Technician;
      8. (h) Medical Technician;
      9. (i) Renal Dialysis Technician (Clinical Renal Physiologist);
      10. (j) Technical Officer;
      11. (k) Welfare Worker;
      12. (l) Youth Worker; and
      13. (m) at the Peter MacCallum Cancer Institute only:
        1. (i) Mechanical Officer;
        2. (ii) Radiation Engineer; and
        3. (iii) Research Technologist (Research Scientist).
    4. 1.4 Allied Health Manager means an employee required to undertake responsibility for the organisation of the department and the supervision of staff and/or to manage a service wide program and who has responsibility for budgets, management of staff, clinical and service outcomes in the program, provision of professional leadership and guidance of staff. An Employee classified in an Allied Health Manager position may be responsible for a program across a number of sites or be responsible for a multi-disciplinary allied health professional structure across a number of sites or a large department/program for a single professional stream. Allied Health Managers must be Employees from an AHP1 Classification.
    5. 1.5 Assistant Allied Health Manager means an employee required to assist and to deputise for an Allied Health Manager. Assistant Allied Health Managers must be Employees from an AHP 1 Classification.
    6. 1.6 Full-Time Employees in the Allied Health Manager and Assistant Allied Health Manager classifications is the effective full-time (i.e. add the number of hours regularly worked by the Employees that report to the Allied Health Manager/Assistant Allied Health Manager and divide by 38 to derive the effective full-time).
    7. 1.7 Health Promotion means the planning, development, implementation and evaluation of Health Promotion policies and projects using a variety of strategies, including health education, mass media, community development and community engagement processes, advocacy and lobbying strategies, social marketing, health policy, and structural and environmental strategies. Workforce development and capacity building strategies are also important components of Health Promotion practice.
    8. 1.8 Teach students is the process of imparting theoretical professional knowledge, such as that provided by a Clinical Educator.
    9. 1.9 Train or Training is generally understood to be the process of imparting practical professional knowledge.

    SECTION B – AHP1 General Classification Descriptors¶

    AHP1 Classifications – General¶

    Application¶

    The AHP1 Classification Descriptors – General apply to all AHP1 Classifications except:

    1. 1.1 Radiation Therapy Technologist (Radiation Therapist) (see Section C of this Appendix 4).
    2. 1.2 Sonographer with respect to Grades 1, 2 and 3 only. The AHP1 Classification Descriptors – General for Grades 4, 5, 6 and 7 apply to Sonographers (see Section D of this Appendix 4 for the Sonographer Grades 1, 2 and 3, and an additional Grade 4 descriptor for Sonographers).

    Intern – Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer) and Nuclear Medicine Technologist only¶

    1. 2.1 This classification applies to Medical Imaging Technologists (MIT) and Nuclear Medicine Technologists (NMT) only.
    2. 2.2 An Intern Medical Imaging Technologist or Nuclear Medicine Technologist is an employee who has provisional registration under the National Registration and Accreditation Scheme with the Medical Radiation Practice Board of Australia and is undertaking a clinical placement following the completion of their qualification.

    Grade 1¶

    Grade 1 – General Definition¶

    1. 3.1 A Grade 1 Employee is an employee who:
      1. (a) has a relevant qualification for their profession and/or meets the entry requirements described at Schedule 1 of this Appendix 4;
      2. (b) works on routine tasks within the scope of practice for their profession, consulting with a more experienced Employee when problems arise or when dealing with matters they are unfamiliar with; and
      3. (c) is able to work with students.
    2. 3.2 This will generally be the entry level for new graduates.
    3. 3.3 A Grade 1 Employee cannot:
      1. (a) supervise students;
      2. (b) Train students;
      3. (c) work shifts that would attract the night shift allowance; and/or
      4. (d) be on-call.
    4. 3.4 AHP1 – Grade 1 to Grade 2 progression
      1. (a) Progression on anniversary - general
        1. (i) Except as provided at subclause 3.4(b) and 3.4(c) of this Appendix 4 date of their commencement in Grade 1, Year 5, that is upon the completion of 12 months at Grade 1 Year 5.
        2. (ii) An Employee who progresses to Grade 2 will, if required, undertake student supervision and Training and work independently for the purposes of undertaking night shift and on-call duties.
      2. (b) Progression on anniversary – exception An Employee as described at subclause 3.4(a) of this Appendix 4 Section B, may have their progression deferred where, subject to the Employer complying with subclause 15.1(a), the Employee has been performance managed in accordance with subclause 15.3 for at least six (6) weeks immediately before the anniversary of their commencement at Grade 1, Year 5, save that:
        1. (i) the Employee will be reclassified to Grade 2 immediately after the performance management ends as the Employee’s performance has reached an acceptable standard. Average performance is not a reason to delay progression; and
        2. (ii) an Employee whose reclassification to Grade 2 has been deferred may invoke the Dispute Resolution Procedure in clause 14. Except where otherwise agreed, if the resolution of the dispute results in reclassification being granted, the reclassification to Grade 2 will be backdated to the anniversary of the Employee’s commencement at Grade 1, Year 5.
      3. (c) Employee elects not to progress to Grade 2
        1. (i) An Employee may notify the Employer in writing that they do not wish to be reclassified to Grade 2.
        2. (ii) Such an Employee may at a later time give written notice to the Employer that they elect to be reclassified to Grade 2 and, where they have been classified at Grade 1 Year 5 for at least 12 months, the Employer will immediately reclassify the Employee to Grade 2, subject to subclause 3.4(b) of this Appendix 4 Section B.
        3. (iii) The Employer must not direct, propose or suggest to an Employee that they elect not to be reclassified to Grade 2.
      4. (d) No requirement for vacancy
        1. (i) The movement of an Employee from Grade 1 to 2 does not rely on a vacancy or funding for the position but will be determined solely in accordance with this subclause 3.4 of this Appendix 4 Section B.
        2. (ii) Notwithstanding the above, a Grade 1 may be reclassified as a Grade 2 by appointment or in accordance with clause 86 (Classification and Reclassification) prior to the anniversary date of their commencement in Grade 1, Year 5.

    Grade 2¶

    1. 4.1 Grade 2 – General Definition (does not apply to MIT)
      1. (a) A Grade 2 Employee is an employee required to undertake additional duties/responsibilities to a Grade 1 Employee, for example:
        1. (i) supervising and Training students;
        2. (ii) working shifts that would attract the night shift allowance;
        3. (iii) being on-call;
        4. (iv) supervising staff including clinical supervision of Grade 1 Employees;
      2. (v) performing work which requires special knowledge or depth of experience. In the case of Cardiac Technologists (Cardiac Physiologist), Medical Librarians, Orthotists/Prosthetists, Physiotherapists, Podiatrists and Social Workers examples of areas in which such work may be performed are listed in Schedule 2 of this Appendix 4;
        1. (vi) being required to take charge of a section of a department;
        2. (vii) holding an equivalent position at a smaller establishment such as a day hospital/centre, nursing home or community health centre;
        3. (viii) in the case of Health Information Manager (Medical Records Administrator) being responsible for clinical trial/data management at recognised trials including national and international trials; and/or
        4. (ix) in the case of Play Therapist, research/case studies, and/or client and group program supervision and/or evaluation.
      3. (b) A Grade 2 Employee also includes an employee who has progressed to Grade 2 from Grade 1 Year 5 in accordance with Appendix 4 Section B subclause 3.4 above, meaning they will, if required, undertake the duties in Appendix 4 Section B subclause 4.1(a) including student supervision and Training, and work independently for the purposes of undertaking night shift and on-call duties.
    2. 4.2 Grade 2 – Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer)
      1. (a) A Grade 2 Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer) is an employee who is required to undertake additional responsibilities and/or who has additional experience who demonstrates a degree of competence and ability to work independently and without supervision which reflects a level of continuing education and/or practical expertise. Parameters for this position would include one (1) or more of the following:
        1. (i) supervising students;
        2. (ii) working shifts that would attract the night shift allowance;
        3. (iii) being on-call;
        4. (iv) a Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer) who is required to supervise other medical imaging staff including clinical supervision of Grade 1 Employees, and Train medical imaging students;
      2. (v) a Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer) who is required to supervise a section of a department;
        1. (vi) holds an equivalent position at a smaller establishment such as a day hospital/centre, nursing home or community health centre; or
        2. (vii) a Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer) who can demonstrate extensive or special knowledge, experience and competence in any of the specialist modalities or areas of additional responsibilities such as computed tomography (CT), digital subtraction angiography (DSA), cardiac angiography, mammography, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), picture archiving and communication systems (PACS), radiology information system (RIS) or quality assurance activities.
      3. (b) A Grade 2 Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer) also includes an employee who has progressed to Grade 2 from Grade 1 Year 5 in accordance with undertake the duties in Appendix 4 Section B subclause 4.2(a) including student supervision and Training and work independently for the purposes of undertaking night shift and on-call duties.
    3. 4.3 Grade 2 – General A Grade 2 Employee cannot Teach students.

    Grade 3¶

    1. 5.1 Grade 3 – General Definition A Grade 3 Employee is an employee who:
      1. (a) in addition to undertaking or having the ability to undertake the Grade 2 duties/responsibilities will:
        1. (i) normally have at least seven (7) years’ experience in the relevant profession; and
        2. (ii) possesses specific knowledge in and works in an area of their profession (clinical, educational, research and/or managerial) recognised as requiring high levels of specialist knowledge;
      2. (b) is an Allied Health Manager or Assistant Allied Health Manager as defined in subclause 1.4 or 1.5 of Section A of this Appendix 4 who meets the requirements of subclause 5.4(b)(i), (ii), (iii) or (iv) of Section B of this Appendix 4; and/or
      3. (c) teaches under-graduate students, post graduate students and/or interns, primarily in a clinical setting.

    In the case of a Health Information Manager (Medical Records Administrator), examples of specialised knowledge are at Schedule 3.

    Note: The experience referred to at subclause 5.1(a)(i) of this Appendix 4 Section B is indicative and is not a requirement for an Employee to be classified at Grade 3. Nothing prevents the appointment of an Employee to Grade 3 with less than seven (7) years’ experience and where an Employee meets the requirements to be classified at Grade 3, they must be classified at Grade 3 even if they do not have seven (7) years’ experience.

    1. 5.2 Role function An Employee in a Grade 3 position performs duties within or across the following areas of expertise:
      1. (a) Clinical;
      2. (b) Managerial;
      3. (c) Education; and/or
      4. (d) Research.
    2. 5.3 Clinical
      1. (a) Indicative duties/responsibilities include:
        1. (i) working in a clinical area of their profession that requires high levels of specialist knowledge;
        2. (ii) clinical supervision of Grade 1 and Grade 2 Employees;
        3. (iii) management of quality improvement;
        4. (iv) acting on expert advisory committees;
      2. (v) providing specialist advice to other Employees or staff in their profession/discipline or other disciplines including secondary consultation; and/or
        1. (vi) having an Advanced Practice role (as defined in clause 1 of Schedule 4 of this Appendix 4) within the level of responsibility appropriate for a Grade 3 Employee as described at Schedule 4 of this Appendix 4.
      3. (b) A Grade 3 Employee whose duties are mostly within the Clinical area of expertise may be described as a Senior Clinician.
    3. 5.4 Managerial
      1. (a) Indicative duties/responsibilities include:
        1. (i) administrative functions;
        2. (ii) mentoring and/or managerial supervision of Employees;
        3. (iii) advocating to more senior management on behalf of their team;
        4. (iv) budget and/or human resource management; and/or
      2. (v) being a manager of a team (discipline specific or multi-disciplinary) including in a community health setting or similar.
      3. (b) A Grade 3 Employee whose duties are mostly within the Managerial area of expertise may be an:
        1. (i) other than for Orthotists/Prosthetists, Allied Health Manager (as defined in subclause 1.4 of Section A of this Appendix 4) Grade 3 in charge of 1 to 14 Full-Time Employees and/or other staff not covered by this Agreement totalling 6 to 25 in number, save that: A. an Allied Health Manager (as defined in subclause 1.4 of Section A of this Appendix 4) Grade 3 in charge of 6 to 14 Full-Time Employees and/or other staff not covered by this Agreement totalling 15 to 25 in number will commence at the Grade 3 Year 4B rate of pay;
        2. (ii) for Orthotists/Prosthetists, Allied Health Manager (as defined in subclause 1.4 of Section A of this Appendix 4) Grade 3 in charge of 1 to 8 Full-Time Employees, save that: A. an Orthotist/Prosthetist Allied Health Manager (as defined in subclause 1.4 of Section A of this Appendix 4) Grade 3 in charge of 4 to 8 Full-Time Employees will commence at the Grade 3 Year 4B rate of pay;
        3. (iii) other than for Medical Imaging Technologists (Radiographers), Assistant Allied Health Manager (as defined in subclause 1.4 of Section A of this Appendix 4) required to assist and deputise for an Allied Health Manager who is in charge of at least 6 Full-Time Employees and/or other staff not covered by this Agreement totalling at least 15 in number;
        4. (iv) for Medical Imaging Technologists (Radiographers), Assistant Allied Health Manager (as defined in subclause 1.4 of Section A of this Appendix 4) required to assist and deputise for an Allied Health Manager who is in charge of 6 to 24 Full-Time Employees and/or other staff not covered by this Agreement totalling 15 to 27 in number, save that: A. a Medical Imaging Technologist Assistant Allied Health Manager (as defined in subclause 1.4 of Section A of this Appendix 4) required to assist and deputise for an Allied Health Manager who is in charge of 15 to 24 Full-Time Employees and/or other staff not covered by this Agreement totalling 26 to 27 in number will commence at the Grade 3 Year 4B rate of pay; or
        5. (v) other Grade 3 managerial role.
    4. 5.5 Education
      1. (a) Indicative duties/responsibilities include:
        1. (i) teaching under-graduate students, post graduate students and/or interns, primarily in a clinical setting;
        2. (ii) lecturing in their clinical speciality;
        3. (iii) providing education to staff from other professions;
        4. (iv) coordination of student placements;
      2. (v) assisting a Grade 4 Clinical Educator (if applicable);
        1. (vi) in the case of a Cardiac Technologist (Cardiac Physiologist), Health Information Manager (Medical Records Administrator), Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer) and Nuclear Medicine Technologist, having a proven record in teaching; and/or
        2. (vii) in the case of Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer), being a clinical educator in a department of less than 25.
      3. (b) A Grade 3 Employee whose duties are mostly within the Education area of expertise may be described as a Clinical Educator.
    5. 5.6 Research
      1. (a) Indicative duties/responsibilities include:
        1. (i) research;
        2. (ii) service development including new practice/s in the profession;
        3. (iii) complex project planning and management;
        4. (iv) contributing to the research program and mentoring staff;
      2. (v) assisting a Grade 4 Researcher (if applicable); and/or
        1. (vi) in the case of a Cardiac Technologist (Cardiac Physiologist), Health Information Manager (Medical Records Administrator), Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer) and Nuclear Medicine Technologist, having a proven record in research.
      3. (b) A Grade 3 Employee whose duties are mostly within the Research area of expertise may be described as an Allied Health Researcher.

    Grade 4¶

    1. 6.1 Grade 4 – General Definition A Grade 4 Employee is an employee who:
      1. (a) in addition to undertaking or having the ability to undertake the Grade 3 responsibilities, has extensive specialised knowledge in their profession or an area of their profession, and/or is at a supervisory level in one or more of the specific areas of their profession which require extensive specialised knowledge and:
        1. (i) would normally have at least 10 years’ experience in the relevant profession; and
        2. (ii) holds significant educational, administrative, managerial, research and/or clinical responsibilities; or
      2. (b) is an Allied Health Manager or Assistant Allied Health Manager as defined in subclause 1.4 or 1.5 of Section A of this Appendix 4 and meets the requirements of subclause 6.4(b)(i), (ii) or (iii) of Section B of this Appendix 4.

    Note: The experience referred to at subclause 6.1(a)(i) of this Appendix 4 Section B is indicative and is not a requirement for an Employee to be classified at Grade 4. Nothing prevents the appointment of an Employee to Grade 4 with less than 10 years’ experience and where an Employee meets the requirements to be classified at Grade 4, they must be classified at Grade 4 even if they do not have 10 years’ experience.

    1. 6.2 Role function An Employee in a Grade 4 position performs duties within or across the following areas of expertise:
      1. (a) Clinical;
      2. (b) Managerial;
      3. (c) Education; and/or
      4. (d) Research.
    2. 6.3 Clinical
      1. (a) Indicative duties/responsibilities include:
        1. (i) being at a supervisory level in one (1) or more clinical areas of their profession;
        2. (ii) being a specialist in a clinical area of their profession which requires extensive specialised knowledge and performance;
        3. (iii) mentoring and/or professional supervision of other Employees;
        4. (iv) having higher academic achievements, such as a post-graduate qualification;
      2. (v) performing an Advanced Practice role (as defined in clause 1 of Schedule 4 of this Appendix 4) within the level of responsibility appropriate for a Grade 4 Employee as described at Schedule 4 of this Appendix 4;
        1. (vi) clinical leadership for a team or stream of care; and/or
        2. (vii) in the case of a Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer) only, is in a large or multi-campus department and is either at a senior level in one (1) or more of the specific branches of their profession which require extensive specialised knowledge and performance or over multiple diagnostic units in the same modality.
      3. (b) A Grade 4 Employee whose duties are mostly within the Clinical area of expertise may be described as a Lead or Advanced Clinician.
    3. 6.4 Managerial
      1. (a) Indicative duties/responsibilities include:
        1. (i) management of a program/s, such as the quality assurance program;
        2. (ii) management/supervision of staff within a program;
        3. (iii) in the case of Medical Imaging Technologist (Radiographer) and Nuclear Medicine Technologist, management of imaging specific computer systems; and/or
        4. (iv) budget and/or human resource management in a department or area that may have a larger number of staff than a department or area managed by a Grade 3 Manager.
      2. (b) A Grade 4 Employee whose duties are mostly within the Managerial area of specialisation may be an:
        1.     Schedule 4 – Advanced Practice Roles, Grades 3 and 4¶

    Definition¶

    Advanced Practice is:

    1. 1.1 work that is within the currently recognised scope of practice for the profession, but that in custom and practice has been performed by other professions or that was previously not within the recognised scope of practice for the profession and is advanced work that requires significant professional experience and competency development, and may require additional training as well; and/or
    2. 1.2 work that is outside the currently recognised scope of practice for the profession and that requires some method of credentialing following additional training and competency development, with appropriate regulatory change where required.

    Application of this Schedule¶

    This Schedule 4 of Appendix 4 applies to all AHP1 Classifications, save that a Sonographer cannot be employed at Grade 3A as a qualified Sonographer is classified at Grade 3 or above.

    Advanced Practice Grade 3¶

    Indicative responsibilities of a Grade 3 Employee performing Advanced Practice would include:

    1. 3.1 exercising professional judgement based on detailed knowledge of the area of expertise;
    2. 3.2 working independently or under the direction or supervision of a Grade 4 Advanced Practitioner, and when problems arise or when dealing with matters they are unfamiliar with, consulting with a Grade 4 Advanced Practitioner where possible or otherwise with a more experienced and suitably qualified health professional;
    3. 3.3 helping develop and/or applying profession principles and new technology and/or knowledge of crucial work which can encompass a single discipline or a variety of disciplines;
    4. 3.4 typically are expected to be experts recognised by the organisation and their peers; and/or
    5. 3.5 demonstrated expert specialist knowledge of contemporary methods, principles and practice and skills across the relevant profession.

    Advanced Practice Grade 4¶

    Indicative responsibilities of a Grade 4 Employee performing Advanced Practice would include:

    1. 4.1 exercising significant professional judgement based on a detailed knowledge of the area of expertise;
    2. 4.2 developing and/or applying profession principles and new technology and/or knowledge of crucial work which can encompass a single discipline or a variety of disciplines;
    3. 4.3 making a significant contribution towards the development and achievement of allied health advanced practice at a local or state-wide level;
    4. 4.4 making independent decisions related to a wide area of expert practice in the relevant field and being responsible for outcomes from the practice of other allied health professionals;
    5. 4.5 requiring expert specialist knowledge of contemporary methods, principles and practice and skills across the area of expertise;
    6. 4.6 typically are expected to be experts recognised in their field by peers and others within and outside the organisation; and/or
    7. 4.7 providing professional and clinical supervision to Grade 3 and 3A Advanced Practitioners and other allied health professionals and other professional staff.

    Who can undertake Advanced Practice and Advanced Practice 3A¶

    1. 5.1 Grade 1 Employees cannot perform Advanced Practice work or undertake training in Advanced Practice.
    2. 5.2 Grade 2 Employees cannot undertake Advanced Practice training, except in the circumstance specified in subclause 5.3 of Schedule 4 of this Appendix 4 below. Grade 2 Employees cannot perform Advanced Practice work, except in the circumstance specified in subclauses 5.4, 5.5 and 5.6 of Schedule 4 of this Appendix 4.
    3. 5.3 Experienced Grade 2 Employees may undertake training in Advanced Practice as must occur under direct and structured supervision, consistent with and appropriate to the training being undertaken, by an appropriately trained and credentialed Advanced Practitioner or other suitably trained and qualified health professional. A Grade 2 Employee can only undertake Advanced Practice training where there is written agreement between the Employer and the Employee that sets out:
      1. (a) a defined training period (which may be varied by agreement) which must be reasonable taking into account the Advanced Practice area that the Employee is undertaking training in and the Employee’s circumstances;
      2. (b) the nature of the training and assessment;
      3. (c) the training content;
      4. (d) supervisory, oversight and monitoring arrangements; and
      5. (e) the anticipated use of the skill once credentialed (for example, participating in a particular type of clinic or exercising a particular skill). Where the Employee has achieved competency in the advanced practice area in which they have been undertaking training, the Employer must credential them. Where the Employee performs the Advanced Practice work after being credentialed they will be paid and classified at the relevant Advanced Practice level as set out in this Agreement at subclause 5.4 of Schedule 4 of this Appendix 4, except where they meet the requirements of AHP1 Grade 3 or 4, in which case they will be classified at AHP1 Grade 3 or 4.
    4. 5.4 A Grade 2 Employee whose role requires them to perform Advanced Practice work will be reclassified as a full-time or part-time Grade 3A Advanced Practitioner on an ongoing basis. Such an Employee must:
      1. (a) have completed credentialing;
      2. (b) be paid a weekly rate of pay that reflects:
        1. (i) 15.2 hours at the relevant Grade 3 rate (pro-rata for part-time Employees); and
        2. (ii) 22.8 hours at the Grade 2 Year 4 rate (pro-rata for part-time Employees); with this rate being the Grade 3A Advanced Practice classification rate which is set out at Part A of Appendix 2 of this Agreement and incremental progression will apply;
      3. (c) work a minimum of 0.4EFT;
      4. (d) perform no more than 15.2 hours (pro-rata for part-time Employees) of Advanced Practice work in any one (1) week. Where such an Employee performs more than 15.2 hours (pro-rata for part-time Employees) of Advanced Practice work in any one (1) week, they will be paid at the relevant Grade 3 rate for the entire week; and
      5. (e) receive the on-going support, training and supervision consistent with the Department framework as part of their ongoing professional development and to support succession planning for moving to an Advanced Practice role.
    5. 5.5 Where a Grade 3A Advanced Practitioner performs Advanced Practice work in a clinic setting:
      1. (a) it is acknowledged that such clinics involve diagnostic, clinical and administrative tasks; and
      2. (b) for the purpose of determining whether the amount of Advanced Practice work performed does not exceed 15.2 hours (see subclause 5.4(d) of Schedule 4 of this Appendix 4), each attendance at a clinic will count as:
        1. (i) half a day (3.8 hours); or
        2. (ii) the length of time spent undertaking the clinic and all tasks associated with the clinic, including those referred to in subclause 5.5(a) of Schedule 4 of this Appendix 4.
    6. 5.6 A Grade 3A Advanced Practitioner will not perform Advanced Practice work on more than three (3) days per week.     8. 5.8 A body agreed by the Employers’ representative and the Union, will look at developing an appropriate pathway to Advanced Practice roles including appropriate proportions of Advanced Practitioners in training to Grade 3 and 4 Advanced Practitioners and monitor the impact and development of Advanced Practice.

      ## Schedule 5 – Award Health Professional Levels 2 and 3 { #sch-5 } ### Level 2 { #sch-5-level-2 }

      For the purposes of the Award references in this Agreement, the current Health Professional Employee Level 2 classification definition is outlined below:

      1. (a) A health professional at this level works independently and is required to exercise independent judgment on routine matters. They may require professional supervision from more senior members of the profession or health team when performing novel, complex, or critical tasks. They have demonstrated a commitment to continuing professional development and may have contributed to workplace education through provision of seminars, lectures or in-services. At this level the health professional may be actively involved in quality improvement activities or research.
      2. (b) At this level the health professional contributes to the evaluation and analysis of guidelines, policies and procedures applicable to their clinical/professional work and may be required to contribute to the supervision of discipline specific students.
      ### Level 3 { #sch-5-level-3 }

      For the purposes of the Award references in this Agreement, the current Health Professional Employee Level 3 classification definition is outlined below:

      1. (a) A health professional at this level would be experienced and be able to independently apply professional knowledge and judgment when performing novel, complex, or critical tasks specific to their discipline. At this level health professionals will have additional responsibilities.
      2. (b) An employee at this level:
        1. (i) works in an area that requires high levels of specialist knowledge and skill as recognised by the employer;
        2. (ii) is actively contributing to the development of professional knowledge and skills in their field of work as demonstrated by positive impacts on service delivery, positive referral patterns to area of expertise and quantifiable/measurable improvements in health outcomes;
        3. (iii) may be a sole discipline specific health professional in a metropolitan, regional or rural setting who practices in professional isolation from health professionals from the same discipline;
        4. (iv) is performing across a number of recognised specialties within a discipline;
      3. (v) may be accountable for allocation and/or expenditure of resources and ensuring targets are met and is responsible for ensuring optimal budget outcomes for their customers and communities;
        1. (vi) may be responsible for providing regular feedback and appraisals for senior staff to improve health outcomes for customers and for maintaining a performance management system; and
        2. (vii) is responsible for providing support for the efficient, cost effective and timely delivery of services.
